Study on Nanocomposites Based on Carbonate@Ceria
Rizwan Raza1/commaori2/commaori∗, Ying Ma 3/commaori4, Xiaodi Wang 3/commaori4, Xiangrong Liu 1/commaori5, and Bin Zhu 1/commaori4/commaori5
1Department of Energy Technology, Royal Institute of Technology (KTH), 10044, Stockholm, Sweden
2Department of Physics, COMSATS Institute of Information Technology, Lahore 54000, Pakistan
3Division of Functional Materials, Royal Institute of Technology (KTH), 16440 Stockholm, Sweden
4College of Material Science and Chemical Engineering, Harbin Engineering University, 150001, China

Nanocomposites based on the ceria-carbonate composite have demonstrated as electrolytes in
development of successful 300–600 /compBC fuel cell technology . In this paper, the nanocomposite electrolyte based on carbonate@SDC (SDC: samarium doped ceria) was directly synthesized from the
co-precipitation method and characterized by XRD, SEM, TEM, BET , etc. It was proved that the
carbonate@SDC was a two-phase material with average particle size about 14.5 nm ( SBET) and
crystalline size ( DXRD) ranged from 12 to 14 nm. When the carbonate@SDC electrolyte was used
to fabricate single SOFC, the cell shows remarkable performance with maximum power density
1000–1180 mw/cm 2 at low temperature (300–550 /compBC).
Keywords: Nanocomposites, Ceria, Sodium Carbonate, Amorphous, Coated Ceria.
1. INTRODUCTION
Solid oxide fuel cells (SOFCs) are considered as one of the
most promising power generation devices. 1/commaori2 Conventional
SOFCs based on yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) as electrolyte has achieved great success in SOFCs technology. 3/commaori4
However, in order to reach high enough oxygen ion conductivity, conventional SOFCs have to be operated at very
high temperature (800 ∼1000 /compBC), a condition that bring a
lot of disadvantages in long-term stability, manufacturing
cost, etc. 3–5 Therefore, reducing the operation temperature
is becoming a world tendency in order to develop marketable SOFCs.3–7 In last several decades, ion-doped CeO 2,
with an order of magnitude higher ionic conductivity than
that of YSZ, has been considered as potential ion electrolyte for low temperature SOFCs (LTSOFCs). 4–7 However, ceria-based oxides show a mixed ionic-electronic
conducting behaviour in reduced environment because of
the reduction of Ce 4+ to Ce 3+, which poses a signiﬁcant
decrease of the cell voltage, power output and efﬁciency. 4
Therefore, the discovery of a new solid electrolyte for lowtemperature SOFCs is a grand challenge for the SOFC
community.1/commaori3
“The nanotechnology world and energy meet to unveil
a realm of functional materials for fuelling the challenge of low-carbon, sustainable energy.” 7 As Schloegl

750 /compBC by a single cell. 11 After several years’ development, Huang et al. reported a remarkable performance of

2. SYNTHESIS OF NANOCOMPOSITES
CARBONATES@CERIA
The nanocomposites based on carbonate@ceria two
phase samples were prepared by one-step co-precipitation
method.19−20 Stoichiometric amount of samarium (III)
nitrate hexahydrate Sm(NO 3/rparenori3 · 6H2O and cerium (III)
nitrate hexahydrate Ce(NO 3/rparenori3· 6H2O were dissolved in the
ionic water to get the 0.5 M solutions with a molar ratio of
Ce3+/colonoriSm3+ is 4:1. 13–19 Then sodium carbonate (Na 2CO3)
solution was added into above cation solution as the precipitation agent under vigorous stirring, with molar ratio,
i.e., (Ce 3++Sm3+):CO2−
3 = 1:2, 1:3 and 1:4 (These samples were named as Ce12, Ce13 and Ce14 respectively).
There is a surplus of sodium carbonate; only a molar
ratio of Sm 3+/Ce3+:CO2−
3 = 1:1.5 is needed to complete
the SDC carbonate deposit formation; thus 0.05, 0.15 and
0.25 mole of Na 2CO3 for Ce12, Ce13 and Ce14, respectively, was unused and remained in the solution together
with the deposit. This mixture of solution and deposit is
then subjected to a vaporizing process, followed by drying and calcination at 800 /compBC for 4 hour, resulting in the
as-prepared SDC-Na 2CO3 composites.13–19

4. ELECTROCHEMICAL MEASUREMENTS
The single cells were fabricated using the hot pressing
process. Equal weights of composite electrolyte and electrode we prepared were ﬁrst mixed to prepare anode material. Then anode, electrolyte and cathode were uniaxially
pressed into a pellet and then sintered at 650 /compBC for 30 min
in air.12–13 The size of the pellets had diameter of 13 mm
and thickness of 0.8 mm. Finally, both anode and cathode
surfaces were painted by silver paste as current collectors for fuel cell measurements. 13–17 The fuel cell with the
active area of 0.7 cm 2 was tested at 400 to 550 /compBCb y
a computerized fuel cell measurement equipment (L-43),
where hydrogen and air were used as fuel and oxidant
respectively. The gas ﬂow rates were controlled in the
range of 80 to 120 ml min −1 at 1 atm pressure.
5. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
In our previous work, we reported the novel coreshell nanostructured carbonate@SDC composite for lowtemperature SOFC applications. 13 In this work we
improved the synthetic processes using a one-step coprecipitation process to prepare carbonate@SDC nanocomposites, which has provided a number of advantages:
(a) to simplify the preparation processes involved to
enhance the production;
(b) to make the produced materials in good quality control, more homogenous composite micro-structure;
(c) to reduce manufacturing costs for material
production.19–20 This work reports the improved carbonate@ceria nanocomposite electrolyte material: structural
and electrical properties with more fundamentally studies
on the carbonate@ceria as the electrolyte.

6. CONCLUSIONS
The nanocomposite study of carbonate@ceria is very supportive to reduce the temperature and comprehend the
two phase electrolytes for solid oxide fuel cell. The
nanocomposite electrolyte based on carbonate@ceria was
directly synthesized from the co-precipitation method. It
was proved that the carbonate@ceria was a two-phase
material with a core-shelled composite structure in nanoscale. This nanocomposite electrolyte with average particle size about 14.5 nm ( DBET) and crystalline size ( DXRD)
ranged from 12 to 14 nm. The maximum power density 1000–1180 mw/cm 2 at low temperature (300–550 /compBC)
shows the substantial best from all SOFC electrolytes
which were reported.
These results show that the NANOCOFC approach of
designing and developing superionic conductors for low
temperature SOFCs makes a feasibility to construct interfaces that may act as “superionic highway” in two-phase
material based on the carbonate coated on SDC while each
constituent individual phase, SDC or Na 2CO3 does not
possess. It has been found that the using our unique technique for two phase nanocomposite materials, the solid
oxide fuel cell can lower operating temperatures, contribute better performance with full advantage of the synergistic effects than conventional materials.
